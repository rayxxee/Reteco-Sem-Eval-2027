\subsection{Analysis of Baseline Behavior}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dominance of Dense Representations:} The frozen dense bi-encoder (\texttt{bge-small}) substantially outperforms all classical lexical baselines, achieving a Macro nDCG@10 of \textbf{0.2431} (a 7$\times$ improvement over BM25's 0.0347). This empirical finding validates the hypothesis formed during EDA: the extreme vocabulary gap (Jaccard overlap $<0.05$) creates a hard performance ceiling for pure keyword matching. Pretrained semantic representations successfully retrieve relevant documents that use domain synonyms or paraphrases absent in the query.
    \item \textbf{Efficacy of Learned Temporal Reranking:} The pointwise LightGBM reranker improves BM25 performance across the \texttt{law} domain dramatically (from 0.0574 to 0.1340, a $+133\%$ relative gain), lifting overall Macro nDCG@10 from 0.0347 to 0.0604. 
    \item \textbf{Feature Importance and Connection to EDA:} Inspecting the split-based feature importance of the trained LightGBM trees revealed that the most influential features were \texttt{reciprocal\_rank} (34\% split share), \texttt{year\_overlap} (26\% split share), \texttt{bm25\_norm} (18\% split share), and \texttt{log\_len\_doc} (12\% split share). This confirms our EDA interpretation: while base BM25 scores identify candidate topical relevance, the explicit temporal match feature (\texttt{year\_overlap}) acts as a crucial filter to promote passages aligned with the query's target era.
    \item \textbf{Failure of Naive Hybrid Fusion:} Unsupervised Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) yielded poor performance (Macro 0.0204). Because BM25 suffered from near-zero recall on many queries, combining BM25's noisy top candidates with the high-precision dense candidates degraded the top-10 list.
\end{enumerate}
