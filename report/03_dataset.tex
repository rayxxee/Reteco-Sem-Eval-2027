\section{Dataset and Quality Checks}
\label{sec:dataset}

The RETECO Track 1 (v1.1) benchmark contains 1.16 million documents spanning 13 thematic domains. Version 1.1 provides an official deduplication mapping that removes approximately 29\% redundant documents present in original web crawls.

\subsection{Domain Selection and Hardware Constraints}
Operating under consumer hardware constraints (CPU-only execution, 8 cores, 16.9 GB RAM, absence of dedicated GPU acceleration), processing the entire 1.16-million document corpus with dense bi-encoders was computationally intractable. To ensure rigorous, reproducible experimentation, we selected three small-to-moderate domains satisfying two criteria: (i) complete self-contained corpora under 45,000 documents, and (ii) sufficient development split volume ($\ge 10$ queries) to compute statistically meaningful macro-averages. The selected domains comprise:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{quant} (Quantitative Finance): Specialized mathematical finance, algorithmic trading, and quantitative risk modeling.
    \item \textbf{law} (Legal Jurisprudence): Legal statutes, judicial procedures, labor standards, and regulatory compliance.
    \item \textbf{workplace} (Professional Practice): Labor relations, organizational workplace policies, and professional ethics.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Label Distribution and Skew:} The relevance judgments exhibit extreme sparsity. Queries pair with between 1 and 5 gold documents (median 2.0). Within a 36,000-document corpus, the positive target ratio is $<0.006\%$, representing an acute needle-in-a-haystack ranking challenge.
    \item \textbf{Data Integrity and Anomalies:} Systematic scanning confirmed zero empty or missing document strings ($\text{len}=0$). Corpus passages range from 12 tokens to over 2,840 tokens, exhibiting pronounced positive skewness (mean length substantially exceeds the median).
    \item \textbf{Corpus Metadata:} All texts originate from English-language Stack Exchange Q\&A communities, exhibiting technical forum prose, community-specific jargon, and variable formatting.
\end{itemize}
